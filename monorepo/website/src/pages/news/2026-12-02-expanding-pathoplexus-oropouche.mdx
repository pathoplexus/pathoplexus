# Expanding Pathoplexus: Oropouche virus added

Today, Pathoplexus launches an additional viral pathogen: Oropouche virus. Oropouche virus (OROV) is a segmented single-stranded RNA virus in the family Peribunyaviridae and genus Orthobunyavirus, first identified in Trinidad and Tobago in 1955. The disease typically causes fever, headache, joint and muscle pain, chills, nausea, vomiting, and rash.

OROV is mainly transmitted through the bites of infected midges, although several mosquito species may also act as vectors. The virus circulates in both forest and urban transmission cycles, involving humans, insects, and possibly animals such as primates and sloths.

Historically, cases were limited to countries near the Amazon rainforest and parts of the Caribbean. However, since late 2023, the number of cases has increased significantly, with outbreaks reported in several South American countries including from areas in the region where it was not previously detected [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/oropouche/hcp/clinical-overview/index.html).

In 2024, concerns increased due to reports of two fatal infections and possible mother-to-child transmission during pregnancy [World Health Organisation](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/oropouche-virus-disease). These developments highlight the changing epidemiology of Oropouche virus disease and the need for enhanced surveillance and research. Unfortunately, there are no known vaccines and medicines for OROV.

Pathoplexus aims to support sequence sharing to improve both research, ongoing surveillance efforts, and response to future outbreaks.

## Data sharing
As with other organisms, you can share Oropouche virus data as [Open](/about/terms-of-use/open-data) immediately or specify that data be [Restricted-Use](/about/terms-of-use/restricted-data) for up to 1 year, before becoming Open. Restricted-Use data is still available but is restricted in how it can be used in publications or preprints. All Open data is sent on to INSDC-member databases.

We hope that by adding Oropouche virus to Pathoplexus we can support the response efforts for future outbreaks as well as improve our understanding of the virus' evolution. In addition, we hope to continue expanding the pathogens that Pathoplexus can support in the future.
