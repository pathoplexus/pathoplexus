# Expanding Pathoplexus: Zika virus added

*By the Pathoplexus Team - 22 August 2026*

Today, Pathoplexus launches an additional viral pathogen: Zika virus. Zika virus (ZIKV) gets its name from Zika Forest in Uganda, where it was first identified in a Rhesus macaque monkey in 1947. It is an enveloped, positive sense, single stranded RNA virus that belongs to the Flaviviridae family and genus Flavivirus. 

It is primarily spread through arthropod vectors (mainly Aedes mosquitoes), sexual intercourse, as well as mother-to-child transmission during pregnancy. 

An infection with ZIKV usually causes mild disease and it is typically asymptomatic. Nevertheless, the infection may lead to rare but severe outcomes, including Guillain-Barré syndrome and other systemic and neurologic sequelae. 

Furthermore, the disease can cause serious [birth defects](https://www.cdc.gov/zika/signs-symptoms/index.html) among children borne to mothers infected with the virus .  

During the 2015 - 2016 outbreak in Southern America, it was declared a Public Health Emergency of Internation Concern (PHEIC) and according to the [World Health Organisation](https://www.who.int/publications/m/item/zika-epidemiology-update-may-2026), although the cases declined from 2017 onwards, ZIKV sporadic outbreaks continue to be detected and transmission remains persistent in several countries. 

Zika virus can be categorised into two major lineages namely; the African and Asian lineages. The differences in the epidemic potential and pathogenicity of these viral lineages and strains remain poorly understood. 

Pathoplexus aims to support sequence sharing to improve both understanding and response.

# Data sharing
As with other organisms, you can share zika data as [**Open**](https://pathoplexus.org/about/terms-of-use/open-data) immediately or specify that data be [**Restricted-Use**](https://pathoplexus.org/about/terms-of-use/restricted-data) for up to 1 year, before becoming Open. 

Restricted-Use data is still available but is restricted in how it can be used in publications or preprints. All Open data is sent on to INSDC-member databases.
